# best ai sdr: how to pick a conversational setter that actually books appointments (and what CloseBot costs)

Most people searching for the best AI SDR are not really shopping for a "SDR." They're shopping for someone—or something—that replies to an inbound lead at 11pm, qualifies it, and puts a call on the calendar before the lead cools off.

That job is narrower than the category name suggests. A large chunk of tools marketed as AI SDRs are outbound email machines: they pull a list, write cold sequences, and measure reply rates. A smaller group goes after inbound conversations—SMS, web chat, WhatsApp, Instagram DMs that land in a CRM inbox—and tries to hold an actual back-and-forth until a meeting is booked.

If your leads arrive as replies rather than as cold lists, the second category is the one that matters, and the pricing model in that category behaves nothing like the first.

## Two different products share one label

Before comparing brands, it helps to split the term:

- **Outbound AI SDR.** Finds prospects, writes and sends cold email or LinkedIn touches, handles replies. Priced per seat, per lead, or per thousands of emails. Success is measured by reply rate and meetings per 1,000 sends.
- **Inbound conversational AI SDR.** Sits in your CRM or website chat, answers leads who already raised their hand, qualifies them, handles objections, follows up, and books. Success is measured by conversation-to-booking rate.

Both are legitimate. They just solve different problems, and a tool that's excellent at one is usually mediocre at the other. Most "best AI SDR" roundups blur the two together and then rank them on feature counts, which is why so many buyers end up with a seat they never use.

There's a third thing worth naming: a lot of what sells as "AI SDR" is really a flow builder with a language model bolted on. Keyword bots with better copy. They can qualify a lead through a decision tree, but they fall apart the moment someone asks something the tree doesn't cover.

## What actually separates a good conversational AI SDR

Five things do most of the work, and none of them show up in a demo reel.

**1. Who owns the channel.** Some tools connect to Instagram or WhatsApp directly. Others connect to your CRM and inherit whatever channels are wired into it. Neither approach is wrong, but it changes your total cost, because the second one assumes you already pay for a CRM.

**2. Whether it reasons or branches.** Objective-driven agents handle unscripted questions and awkward replies. Flow builders route. The difference shows up in week three, not in the first conversation.

**3. How messages are billed.** This is where the category gets weird. Some vendors charge per message on top of a platform fee. Some bundle messages into the base price. Some let you rebill the per-message cost to clients. A $64 plan and a $397 plan can end up costing the same, or wildly different, depending on which model you're on.

**4. Failure behaviour.** Every AI setter will eventually hit a question it can't answer, a calendar that errors out, or a model that times out. What it does then—invent an answer, drop the lead, or flag a human—decides whether you keep the client.

**5. Whether you can test before going live.** Conversation testing, rollback, and pause-for-human-takeover are not premium features. They're the difference between shipping an agent and babysitting one.

**Does your lead volume justify a platform fee?** CloseBot's free tier caps at 100 messages a month, which is enough to judge conversation quality against real leads but not enough to run a pipeline. If you're consistently pushing past that, you're looking at the Core track or above.

**Are you selling AI to clients or using it yourself?** If you resell, the Agency plan's $397/mo (or about $331/mo annually) is a different proposition entirely—the rebilling and white-labeling turn a software cost into a revenue line. If you're using it for your own pipeline, the Core tier with metered message volume usually fits better, and Growth only makes sense once you need HIPAA, SLAs, or quarterly audits.

**Where do your leads actually talk to you?** If the honest answer is "Instagram DMs and I don't run a CRM," CloseBot is the wrong shape for you and adding a CRM to fix that is a detour. If it's "SMS, web chat, and my CRM inbox," you're exactly who this was built for.
